Colour gamut and sRGB

Why the same file looks different on two screens, and why sRGB is the web default.

Why the same file looks different on two screens, and why sRGB is the web default.

A gamut is the range of colours a device or colour space can represent. sRGB is narrow but universal; Display P3 is wider and shows more saturated greens and reds. A wide-gamut file shown by sRGB-only software looks oversaturated; the reverse looks dull.

You meet it with P3 phone photos uploaded to a website, fluorescent design colours that cannot be printed, or two laptops showing the same file differently.

Our tools process and export in sRGB, which suits the web, social media and office documents. For print or wide-gamut delivery, finish colour management in a professional editor.

Common misconception: a wider gamut is always better. On the web, colours outside sRGB simply disappear on many devices.
