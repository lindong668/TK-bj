How to Redact a Photo: Blur, Pixelate or Block — What Actually Protects Privacy

You need to share a photo that contains a face, a licence plate or a delivery label. Covering it with a mosaic feels safe — but the three common methods are not equally safe, and the most popular one is the weakest. This guide explains why, and what to do instead.

The three ways people hide things

  • Pixelation (mosaic) — the area is divided into blocks and each block is filled with one averaged colour. Visually "obviously edited", and the default choice in most editors.
  • Blur — each pixel is averaged with its neighbours, so detail is smoothed away. Looks softer and less deliberate.
  • Solid block — the area is painted over with a flat colour. The only method that removes information completely.

All three hide information from a casual glance. They differ enormously in whether the information is merely hidden or genuinely destroyed.

Why mosaic is the weakest option

Pixelation is a downsampling operation: the original block of, say, 16×16 pixels is replaced by a single average colour. That is lossy, but it is also highly structured and predictable — and predictable operations can be partly run backwards.

Researchers and hobbyists have published "depixelation" techniques that reconstruct plausible originals from mosaics, especially when:

  • the blocks are large and evenly aligned to the original pixel grid;
  • the subject is a face — human observers and models are both very good at filling in faces, and even a rough reconstruction may be recognisable;
  • the same image also exists unredacted somewhere else online, giving a reference to match against.

Key point: pixelation hides detail, it does not reliably destroy it. Blur removes far more information because the averaging is overlapping and irreversible, and a solid block removes all of it.

Method comparison

MethodInformation destroyedReconstruction riskBest used for
PixelationPartialMedium to highCasual sharing, non-sensitive content
Blur (large radius)MostLowFaces, backgrounds, general redaction
Solid blockAllNoneDocument numbers, addresses, barcodes, plates

Rule of thumb: if getting it wrong would matter — a legal document, a child's face, a medical record — use a solid block. If the goal is simply "don't make this person the focus", a heavy blur is fine.

Five mistakes that leave identity behind

  • Covering only the eyes. A face is still identifiable from hairline, ears, jaw, glasses, clothing and background. Cover the whole head plus a margin.
  • Using a semi-transparent overlay. A 50% black rectangle leaves the underlying shapes clearly visible, and brightening the image often recovers them.
  • Zooming in only on the crop. The surrounding scene may still identify a person: their house, street sign, school uniform or car.
  • Forgetting the metadata. The photo's EXIF data can contain the GPS coordinates of where it was taken, plus the exact time and device model. Blurring the picture does nothing about that.
  • Exporting from the wrong file. Re-saving a compressed JPEG of an already-compressed JPEG is fine for privacy, but check you are not uploading the original by accident — many chat apps and cloud albums upload full-quality originals even when you previewed a thumbnail.

A short checklist that actually works

  • Cover the entire sensitive object with a margin around it — not just the "identifying part".
  • Prefer a solid block for text, numbers and plates; a heavy blur for faces and scenes.
  • Apply the redaction after any resizing, so the effect is not weakened by scaling.
  • Strip the EXIF data before sharing, particularly the GPS fields.
  • Zoom in on the finished image at 100% and check it from arm's length and close up.
  • Save as a new file, so the untouched original stays private on your own device.

Frequently asked questions

Is blurring a face enough to protect someone's identity?
Usually yes, if the blur radius is large and covers the whole head. A light blur is not enough — details still show through. For anything legally sensitive, use a solid block instead.
Can a pixelated image really be restored?
Partially. Published depixelation methods reconstruct a plausible version of the original rather than an exact copy, but for faces that is often enough to identify someone. Treat mosaic as concealment, not erasure.
Does a screenshot count as redaction?
No. A screenshot of a photo still contains the full image. It only removes metadata — and if the original file is shared separately, the metadata goes with it.
Do I need to worry about the location data?
Yes. Many phones embed GPS coordinates in every photo. A blurred picture shared with intact EXIF can still reveal exactly where it was taken. Strip metadata before sharing.
Why does my blurred area look sharp again after I resize the image?
Because resizing resamples the pixels. Always apply blur as the final step, after all resizing and cropping is finished.

图片打码：马赛克、模糊、纯色块，哪种才真的安全

想发一张照片，里面有车牌、人脸或者快递单——随手加个马赛克就觉得安全了。但三种常见手法的保护强度差很多，而最流行的那种恰恰是最弱的。这篇讲清楚为什么，以及该怎么做。

三种常见遮挡方式

  • 马赛克（像素化）：把区域切成小方块，每块填一个平均色。视觉上「一看就是处理过」，是多数编辑器的默认选项。
  • 模糊：每个像素和周围像素做平均，细节被抹平。看起来更柔和，不那么刻意。
  • 纯色块：直接用纯色涂掉。唯一能把信息彻底清除的方法。

三种都能挡住「随便一眼」，但它们差别巨大：有的只是把信息藏起来，有的是真的销毁掉。

为什么马赛克是最弱的一种

马赛克本质上是一次降采样：原本 16×16 的像素块被压成一个平均色。这确实有损，但它结构规整、规律可预测——而规律性的运算是可以被部分反推的。

已经公开的「去马赛克」技术在这些情况下尤其有效：

  • 色块大而规整，和原图像素网格对齐；
  • 被遮挡的是人脸——人和模型都极擅长补全人脸，哪怕还原得粗糙也足够辨认；
  • 同一张图的未打码版本在网上还能找到，可以直接对照匹配。

要点：马赛克只是藏住细节，并不能可靠地销毁它。模糊因为用的是重叠平均、不可逆，抹掉的信息多得多；纯色块则是全部清除。

三种方式对比

方式信息销毁程度被还原风险适合场景
马赛克部分中到高随手分享、非敏感内容
模糊（大半径）大部分低人脸、背景、一般性遮挡
纯色块全部无证件号、地址、条形码、车牌

经验法则：如果出错的代价很大——法律文件、小孩的脸、病历——用纯色块。如果只是想「别让这个人成为焦点」，重度模糊就够了。

五个会泄露身份的常见错误

  • 只挡眼睛。发际线、耳朵、下颌、眼镜、衣服和背景照样能认出人。要连整个头部带一圈余量一起挡。
  • 用半透明遮罩。50% 透明的黑块底下轮廓依然清楚，把图调亮往往就能还原。
  • 只看局部裁切。画面环境本身就能定位到人：他家、路牌、校服、车子。
  • 忘了元数据。照片的 EXIF 里可能有GPS 坐标，外加精确时间和设备型号。把画面模糊掉对此毫无作用。
  • 发错了文件。很多聊天和云相册即便你预览的是缩略图，上传的仍是原图——发之前确认你选的是处理后的那张。

一份真正有用的检查清单

  • 把敏感物体整个盖住并留一圈余量，不要只挡「有特征的那部分」。
  • 文字、数字、车牌优先纯色块；人脸和场景用重度模糊。
  • 打码放在缩放之后做，避免缩放把效果削弱。
  • 分享前清掉 EXIF，尤其 GPS 字段。
  • 成品放大到 100% 看一遍，远看和凑近看都要检查。
  • 另存为新文件，原始未处理版本只留在自己设备上。

常见问题

把脸模糊掉，就足够保护身份了吗？
通常够，前提是模糊半径够大、并且盖住整个头部。轻微模糊不够，细节仍然透得出来。涉及法律风险的场合请用纯色块。
马赛克真的能被还原吗？
能还原一部分。公开的还原方法得到的是「合理的推测版本」而非精确原图，但对人脸来说往往已经足够辨认。把马赛克理解为「遮蔽」而不是「清除」。
截图算不算打码？
不算。截图后的图片内容完整，只是去掉了元数据。而且如果原文件被单独分享，元数据会跟着一起走。
位置信息需要担心吗？
需要。多数手机会给每张照片写入 GPS 坐标。画面打码了但 EXIF 没清，拍摄地点照样会泄露。分享前先清元数据。
为什么缩放之后模糊区域又变清楚了？
因为缩放会重新采样像素。模糊一定要放在所有缩放、裁剪做完之后的最后一步。
