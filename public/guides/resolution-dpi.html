Resolution, DPI and Print Size: How Pixels Relate to Sharpness

"Make it 300 DPI" may be the most misunderstood request in imaging. This guide explains pixels, resolution, PPI and DPI, and why simply changing the DPI value never makes an image sharper.

Four terms people mix up

  • Pixel — the smallest unit of an image. 1920×1080 means 1920 pixels across and 1080 down. This is the real substance of an image.
  • Resolution — usually means the pixel total (1920×1080), though it is sometimes used loosely for pixel density.
  • PPI — pixels per inch, a density measure used for screens.
  • DPI — dots per inch, originally a printer's ink dot density. In digital images it is routinely confused with PPI.

Key point: total pixel count is what determines sharpness. DPI is only a note about how large the image is intended to be printed.

Why changing DPI does not make an image sharper

A 1920×1080 image has the same pixels whether its DPI flag says 72 or 300. Not one pixel is added. Changing DPI only alters the conversion ratio:

  • 1920 px wide at 72 DPI prints about 67.7 cm wide — large but sparse.
  • 1920 px wide at 300 DPI prints about 16.3 cm wide — small but dense.

Same image: printed small it looks sharper, printed large it looks softer — only the size changed, not the information. To genuinely gain sharpness you need more pixels, which usually means reshooting or regenerating.

The conversion formulas

  • Centimetres = pixels ÷ DPI × 2.54
  • Pixels = centimetres ÷ 2.54 × DPI

For a 10×15 cm photo at 300 DPI you need:

  • Width: 10 ÷ 2.54 × 300 ≈ 1181 px
  • Height: 15 ÷ 2.54 × 300 ≈ 1772 px

So roughly two megapixels is enough for a standard 6-inch print.

Screen and print have completely different demands

  • On screen — only screen pixels matter. Phone displays are roughly 1080–1440 px wide, so a 1500–2000 px long edge is already sharp on a phone. Web images at 1200–1920 px are plenty.
  • In print — 300 DPI is needed to avoid visible grain. A full-bleed A4 needs about 2480×3508, roughly 8.7 megapixels.

So "is this sharp enough" always depends on the purpose — an image fine for social media will usually look soft printed at A4.

Can enlarging make an image sharper?

Strictly, no. Enlarging invents pixels — detail that never existed can only be guessed from surrounding pixels.

  • Traditional interpolation (bilinear, bicubic) just smooths edges; it adds no detail and turns mushy when pushed.
  • Smart upscaling uses a model to infer detail. On faces, buildings and text it can look convincingly sharper, but what it produces is inference, not real detail.

Practical advice: modest enlargement (up to 2×) with a smart tool is acceptable; for large enlargements, find a higher-quality original.

FAQ about resolution and DPI

Does setting DPI to 300 make it print sharply?
No. Changing DPI only edits a flag; the pixel count is unchanged and sharpness does not improve. What matters is total pixels ÷ print size.
Can a 72 DPI image ever be printed?
Certainly. If it has enough pixels (say 4000×3000) it will print sharply even flagged at 72 DPI, because the pixel total is sufficient. The DPI flag only affects the default print size.
How large can a phone photo print?
A 12-megapixel shot (about 4000×3000) prints at roughly 34×25 cm at 300 DPI — slightly larger than A4 — with good quality.
Do web images need 300 DPI?
No. Screens only care about pixels and the DPI flag has no effect on web display. Size web images by pixel dimensions; a 1200–1920 px long edge is usually enough.
Why does an image blur when enlarged?
Because enlarging adds no real detail — it spreads existing pixels and interpolates the gaps. The pixel total is fixed, so enlarging necessarily reduces detail per unit area.

分辨率、DPI 与打印尺寸：像素和清晰度到底是什么关系

「把这张图改成 300 DPI」——这可能是图片领域最常被误解的要求。这篇文章把像素、分辨率、DPI、PPI 的关系讲透，并解释为什么单纯改 DPI 数值不会让图变清晰。

四个名词，别再混着用

  • 像素（Pixel）：图片的最小单位。1920×1080 就是说这张图横向有 1920 个、纵向有 1080 个像素点。这是图片的「真材实料」。
  • 分辨率：通常就指像素总量（如 1920×1080），有时也指单位长度的像素密度，日常说法比较混。
  • PPI：每英寸多少个像素，屏幕用的密度单位。
  • DPI：每英寸多少个点，原本是打印机的墨点密度。数码图片里常被当成 PPI 混用。

要点：像素总数才是决定清晰度的根本，DPI 只是一个「这张图打算印多大」的标注值。

为什么改 DPI 不会让图变清晰

一张 1920×1080 的图，无论把 DPI 标成 72 还是 300，像素一个都没多。改 DPI 只是改变了「每英寸塞多少像素」这个换算比例：

  • 1920 px 宽，按 72 DPI 打印 → 宽约 67.7 cm（大但稀）
  • 1920 px 宽，按 300 DPI 打印 → 宽约 16.3 cm（小但密）

同一张图，印得小就更清晰，印得大就更模糊——变的只是尺寸，信息量没变。想真正变清晰，必须增加像素，而这在多数情况下意味着重新拍或重新生成。

换算公式

  • 厘米 = 像素 ÷ DPI × 2.54
  • 像素 = 厘米 ÷ 2.54 × DPI

举例：要印一张 10×15 cm、300 DPI 的照片，需要的像素是：

  • 宽：10 ÷ 2.54 × 300 ≈ 1181 px
  • 高：15 ÷ 2.54 × 300 ≈ 1772 px

也就是说，约 200 万像素的图就够印一张标准的 6 寸照片了。

屏幕显示和打印，要求完全不同

  • 屏幕看：只需要匹配屏幕像素。主流手机屏宽约 1080–1440 px，所以长边 1500–2000 px 的图在手机上已经足够清晰。网页配图长边 1200–1920 px 就够了。
  • 打印看：需要 300 DPI 才不显颗粒。A4 满幅打印需要约 2480×3508，也就是 870 万像素。

所以「这张图够不够清晰」必须先问用途——发朋友圈够用的图，印成 A4 大概率会糊。

放大图片能变清晰吗

严格说不能。放大的本质是凭空补像素——原本没有的细节，算法只能靠周围像素推测。

  • 传统插值（双线性、双三次）：只是让边缘平滑，不会增加细节，放太大就糊。
  • 智能放大：用模型推测细节，对人脸、建筑、文字这类有规律的内容效果不错，能「看起来」更清晰，但生成的是推测内容，不是真实细节。

实用建议：小幅放大（2 倍以内）用智能放大效果可接受；需要大幅放大的话，最好找原始高清素材。

关于分辨率与 DPI 的常见问题

把 DPI 改成 300 就能打印清晰了吗？
不能。改 DPI 只是改了一个标注值，像素总数没变，清晰度不会变。真正决定打印质量的是像素总数 ÷ 打印尺寸。
72 DPI 的图一定不能打印吗？
不一定。如果这张图像素足够多（比如 4000×3000），即使标注 72 DPI，实际打印出来依然清晰——因为像素总量够。DPI 标注只影响默认打印尺寸。
手机拍的照片能印多大？
以 1200 万像素（约 4000×3000）为例，300 DPI 下可印约 34×25 cm，大致是 A4 略大的尺寸，质量良好。
网页图片需要 300 DPI 吗？
不需要。屏幕只认像素，DPI 标注对网页显示没有任何影响。网页配图按Pixel size考虑即可，长边 1200–1920 px 通常足够。
为什么图片放大后变模糊？
因为放大并没有增加真实细节，只是把原有像素摊开并插值填补空隙。像素总量是固定的，放大必然降低单位面积的细节密度。
