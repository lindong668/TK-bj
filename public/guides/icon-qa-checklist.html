The five field tests in this series produce a lot of conclusions. This page collapses them into one sheet you can actually use: twelve cells, each with a pass example and a fail example, each tied to a specific tool on this site. Print it, tick it, and only then upload the icon set.

It is also the page that keeps the rest of the series honest. A guide that ends with "hope it looks good" is not an acceptance test. A checklist with a defined failure condition is.

How to use the sheet

Load the specimen logo into the ICO & Favicon Generator, export all six sizes, then walk the twelve cells in order with your icon. Keep two browser windows open: one with a white page, one with a near-black page. Most of the twelve checks are just "look at both".

A cell fails if you cannot tell what the mark is, or if any part of it is clipped, haloed, or invisible. Do not average the results. One failed cell means the set is not ready; the fix is listed next to it.

Cell 01 — 16 px silhouette

Look at: the icon at 16 × 16 on a light tab. Fail if: the letterform collapses into a coloured blob, or two adjacent shapes merge. A 16 × 16 bitmap has 256 pixels total; there is no budget for thin strokes, tiny text, faces, QR codes or gradients. Fix: draw the 16 px version separately with a bold silhouette; do not shrink a 512 px master. Why: part 1, the 16 px budget.

Cell 02 — 32 px detail

Look at: a Retina tab or the Windows taskbar. Fail if: small accent elements vanish or the edge is jagged. Fix: include a real 32 px ICO entry, authored at 32 px. Why: rows 4 and 5 of the fourteen-placement table. Tool: ICO & Favicon Generator.

Cell 03 — dark mode

Look at: a near-black tab (#202124). Fail if: a dark glyph disappears. Fix: give the mark a filled backing square whose luminance contrasts with both light and dark chrome. Why: dark mode is a contrast problem, not a second-file problem. Tool: Colour Adjust to test contrast variants before re-exporting.

Cell 04 — light mode

Look at: a white tab / white Explorer view. Fail if: a white or pale glyph disappears. This is the mirror of cell 03 and the reason a mark must pass four swatches: #ffffff, #f1f3f4, #202124, #000000. Fix: same backing-square approach. Why: fourteen placements, cells 2 and 6.

Cell 05 — rounded mask

Look at: an iOS add-to-home-screen preview. Fail if: content near the four corners is cut off. Fix: keep all meaningful content inside a centred circle about two-thirds of the canvas; let the background bleed to the edges. Why: iOS applies a squircle mask to apple-touch-icon. Tool: Rounded Corners to preview the crop.

Cell 06 — transparent edge

Look at: the feathered soft-alpha specimen or your own anti-aliased edge on both black and white. Fail if: a uniform dark ring follows the edge, or dark triangles appear at corners. Fix: re-encode a clean RGBA PNG. Why: failure 4 and 5 in the transparency accident report. Tool: Convert Format.

Cell 07 — no thin lines

Look at: the 16 px render again, specifically any 1-pixel stroke. Fail if: the stroke breaks into dashes or disappears. Fix: thicken the stroke to at least 2 px at the target size, or remove it. Why: downsampling discards sub-pixel detail; the adjustment curve lab shows what happens to values at the extremes.

Cell 08 — no tiny text

Look at: any lettering other than a single bold initial. Fail if: words are unreadable. Fix: replace text with a symbol. A favicon is not a business card. Why: cell 01's arithmetic. Tool: ICO & Favicon Generator, 16 px preview.

Cell 09 — square crop

Look at: the source artwork. Fail if: the source is a tall screenshot or a wide photo and the interesting part is off-centre. Fix: drag the square selection onto the part you want before generating; do not stretch the whole image into a square. Why: our generator's crop step exists for exactly this. Tool: ICO & Favicon Generator or Smart Crop.

Cell 10 — no halo

Look at: the edge on a light background after any corner or blur operation. Fail if: a dirty fringe survives. Fix: re-encode once and re-check; if it persists, re-export from the source editor with straight alpha. Why: failure 4, premultiplied alpha. Tool: Convert Format.

Cell 11 — ICO really has six sizes

Look at: the ICO directory, not the filename. Fail if: the file is a renamed PNG with a single image. Fix: export with all six sizes ticked — 16 / 32 / 48 / 64 / 128 / 256. Why: sizes="any" is only an honest claim if the container holds multiple entries, and Explorer's 256 view exposes the lie. Tool: ICO & Favicon Generator; compare with the known-good six-size sample.

Cell 12 — HTML tags pasted and live

Look at: the deployed page source and a direct request to each href. Fail if: any declared href 404s, or /favicon.ico goes through a redirect chain, or the manifest still lists the old filename. Fix: paste the complete generated snippet, give filenames a version suffix, keep the root ICO as a direct 200. Why: rounds A–D of the priority test. Tool: ICO & Favicon Generator snippet + Base64 Converter for byte checks.

The pass/fail rule, written down

  • Pass: all twelve cells readable and intact on both light and dark backgrounds, with a six-entry ICO and live tags.
  • Conditional pass: one cell fails for a platform you do not support (for example, no iOS home-screen shortcut). Record the exception in the release note.
  • Fail: any of cells 01, 03, 04, 06, 11 or 12 fails. These are the ones that produce visible breakage or no icon at all.

Writing the rule down matters more than the individual cells. A checklist without a decision rule becomes a decorative list.

A release note you can copy

Icon set v2 checked against the 12-cell QA sheet on 2026-11-20. Six-size ICO regenerated. PNG filenames versioned to -v2; apple-touch-icon, manifest array and mask-icon updated in the same commit. Verified in fresh browser profiles on light and dark tabs, plus one iOS add-to-home-screen. Known exception: none.

That note is short, dated and falsifiable. It is also the kind of first-hand record that makes a site worth returning to, which is why we publish our own lab changelog rather than silently editing pages.

Keeping the sheet useful over time

A checklist rots if nobody maintains it. Three habits keep it accurate without turning it into a chore:

  1. Re-run the sheet whenever a browser or OS major version changes the icon pipeline. The delete-one-file-at-a-time test is the ten-minute version of that check.
  2. Record the date and the tool versions in the release note. A year later, "it passed" is meaningless; "it passed on 2026-11-20 with these versions" is evidence.
  3. Add new cells when a new failure mode shows up, and say what was added in the changelog. The sheet grows by observed failures, not by guesswork.

That maintenance habit is the reason this site groups its long-form writing into three labelled series — Rendering Field Tests, Failure Forensics and Checklists — instead of publishing an undifferentiated pile of posts. Each new piece has a place, and each place has a reader who knows what to expect. The guides index lists all of them.

Appendix U — Scoring card

Twelve equal ticks are easy to game, so here is a weighted version. Record a score on the release note rather than a vague "looks fine".

CellsWeightWhy
01, 03, 04×3Unreadable or invisible at the two most common backgrounds
06, 10, 11×2Edge damage and a fake multi-size container produce visible breakage
12×2A dead href means no icon at all on some pages
02, 05, 07, 08, 09×1Important, but usually confined to one placement

Maximum score is 26. Release threshold: 24 or higher, with zero failures in the ×3 and ×2 groups. A mark that fails cell 01 cannot be compensated by passing cells 07 and 08, because those are the same underlying problem seen twice. Writing the weights down prevents the most common failure of a checklist: ticking everything because nothing looks catastrophically broken.

Appendix W — How to log a failure properly

When a cell fails, capture five things before changing anything:

  1. Which cell and which background it failed on.
  2. The file name and its byte size — 19,822 bytes for our known-good ICO, for comparison.
  3. The host and version (browser, OS, device).
  4. A screenshot taken before the fix, on both light and dark pages.
  5. The one change you are about to make, written before you make it.

That discipline is what turns a one-off annoyance into a new cell or a new round in a field test. The Lab Changelog records additions to this sheet for exactly that reason: the sheet grows from observed failures, not from a brainstorm.

Appendix X — One-page print version

If you want the sheet on paper, the twelve checks reduce to twelve lines:

  1. 16 px silhouette readable? 2. 32 px detail intact? 3. Dark tab visible? 4. Light tab visible? 5. Corners survive the mask? 6. Transparent edge clean on black and white? 7. No thin strokes? 8. No tiny text? 9. Square crop on the right subject? 10. No halo? 11. ICO has six directory entries? 12. Every declared href returns 200?

Tick each, sum the weighted score from Appendix U, date it, and file it. That is the whole pre-launch review. The detailed cell descriptions above exist to explain *why* a tick is or is not justified — the print version exists so the review actually happens.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my logo look bad at 16 pixels?

A 16 × 16 icon has 256 pixels total. Thin lines, small text and gradients cannot survive that budget. Draw a separate, bolder 16 px version.

How do I check whether an ICO really contains multiple sizes?

Inspect the ICO directory entries, or compare against a known-good six-size file. A renamed single PNG will look fine in a tab and fail in Explorer's large-icon view.

Should the checklist be re-run after a browser update?

Yes, at least the cells covering icon selection and dark mode. The ten-minute deletion test in part 2 is the fast version.

What counts as a pass?

All twelve cells readable on both light and dark backgrounds, a six-entry ICO, and every declared href returning 200. Any failure in cells 01, 03, 04, 06, 11 or 12 blocks the release.

Can I skip the iOS cell if I do not target mobile?

You can, but record it as a dated exception in the release note rather than silently ignoring it.
