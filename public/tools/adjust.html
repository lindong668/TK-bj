How to adjust colours

  1. Upload the photo you want to adjust
  2. Drag the sliders: brightness controls overall lightness, contrast controls punch, saturation controls vividness
  3. Fix colour casts with temperature; if a photo looks flat, raise contrast or adjust gamma
  4. Tap "Apply" to see the result. Not happy? Change the values and redo — the original is never touched

How it works

Brightness adds or subtracts the same amount from every pixel's RGB values. Simple and direct, but it easily blows out highlights and crushes shadows to black.

Contrast uses a linear mapping: new = factor × (old − 128) + 128. When the factor exceeds 1, bright areas get brighter and dark ones darker, and the image instantly gains "punch".

Saturation first computes each pixel's luma (0.299R + 0.587G + 0.114B), then pushes the colour away from or toward that grey point — which is why −100 gives a fully greyscale image.

Sharpening uses USM (unsharp mask): make a blurred copy, then amplify differences using "original + (original − blur) × strength", which makes edges crisper.

FAQ about colour adjustment

Can I undo a bad adjustment?

Yes. Everything applies to a copy — the original is untouched. Just reset the sliders to 0 or re-upload.

What is the difference between gamma and brightness?

Brightness shifts everything uniformly and tends to lose detail at the extremes. Gamma is non-linear: it lifts midtones mainly, with little effect on highlights and shadows, so it looks more natural on flat photos.

Why does sharpening increase noise?

Sharpening amplifies differences between pixels, and noise is itself a difference, so it gets amplified too. If a photo is noisy, run it through the Denoise tool first.

Does colour adjustment reduce quality?

Exporting to PNG is lossless. But adjusting does change pixel values, and extreme settings (such as max brightness) lose detail irreversibly — make several small adjustments instead.
