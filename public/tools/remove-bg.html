How to remove a background

  1. Upload: Click the dashed area to choose a file, or drag an image straight in. PNG, JPG and WebP are supported; under 5 MB is recommended.
  2. Choose what to do with the background: Keep transparency, fill with white or choose any colour. To keep transparency, download as PNG.
  3. Start: Tap "Remove background"; processing runs in your browser and usually finishes within seconds.
  4. Compare and download: Compare the result with the original and tap "Download result" when happy. Results are not stored on a server, so save yours promptly.

Tip: All processing happens in your browser: images are never uploaded to a server. Results live only on this page, so download them promptly.

How it works

Cut-out work is essentially deciding whether each pixel belongs to the subject or the background. This tool samples the colours at the four corners and edge midpoints, averages them into a background reference colour, then measures each pixel's distance from it in RGB space: within the tolerance (30 by default) it becomes fully transparent background, between one and two times the tolerance it is treated as a transition edge with partial transparency, and everything else stays opaque.

This is colour-difference analysis — no model required, so it is fast and there is no need to upload anything.

Its limits are equally clear: it only works well when the background is fairly uniform and clearly different from the subject. Gradients, hair, feathers and glass — edges that are semi-transparent or extremely detailed — are hard for colour-difference methods, often leaving background residue or eating into the subject.

FAQ about background removal

Why is my result not clean?
The most common cause is a background that is not solid. Gradients, shadows and patterns make the corner samples unrepresentative, and a subject close in colour to the background gets removed along with it. Reduce background interference first, or start from a cleaner original.
Which format should I download for transparency?
It must be PNG. JPG has no alpha channel, so transparent areas get filled with white or black — which is why some people find their transparency "turned white".
Can I switch straight to a red or blue backdrop?
Yes. Choose "Custom colour" in the background options, pick your colour, then start. Common ID photo values are around #FF0000 for red, #438EDB for blue and #FFFFFF for white.
Will my photo be uploaded?
No. Everything runs in your browser and the image data never leaves your device. Results vanish when you close the page; no copy is kept on any server.
