How to make an ID photo

  1. Upload a front-facing photo without a hat (a plain background works best)
  2. Pick a size: 1-inch 295×413, 2-inch 413×579, or a visa format
  3. Choose "AI cut-out and backdrop" and select white, red or blue
  4. Tap "Generate", preview, then download

How it works

Swapping a backdrop means separating the person from the background — technically, image segmentation. This tool prefers an in-browser neural model that understands "where the person is" rather than just looking at colour.

The model outputs a mask: white for the subject, black for the background. Applying that mask as the alpha channel makes the background transparent, and your chosen solid colour is laid underneath.

If the AI model fails to load due to network issues, it falls back automatically to a local algorithm (GrabCut plus edge feathering). It still handles solid backgrounds, just less well on complex ones.

The last step scales proportionally to the target size and centres with padding, so head proportions meet ID photo requirements without distorting the face.

FAQ about ID photo making

There is a white fringe around the hair — what can I do?

That is a common cut-out residue. Process the image with the Background Remover first and raise the tolerance, or choose a backdrop close to the original background to reduce contrast.

Does AI cut-out need the internet?

The first run downloads a model file of a few MB; the browser caches it afterwards. If loading fails it falls back to the local algorithm, so the feature never breaks.

Will my photo be uploaded?

No. Cut-out, backdrop swap and cropping all happen in your browser — the photo never leaves your device.

Why is the size different from what I need?

Different organisations have slightly different requirements (both 295×413 and 260×378 are called "1-inch"). Always follow your issuing authority's official spec, and use the custom size field to enter it exactly.

Can it auto-position the head?

This version centres with padding. If the head sits too high or low, recompose with the Crop tool first.
