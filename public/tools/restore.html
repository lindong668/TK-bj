How to restore old photos

  1. Upload a scanned old photo
  2. Keep "Auto levels" on — it is the key step for fixing faded, flat-looking scans
  3. Set denoise strength by how grainy it is; 2–4 for scans
  4. Add sharpening for crisper outlines, then click "Restore"

How it works

The most common problem with old photos is a flat, grey look: over time the blacks are no longer black and the whites no longer white, everything squeezed into the midtones.

Auto levels builds a histogram per colour channel, clips the darkest and brightest 0.5% of pixels, then stretches the remaining range linearly to 0–255. That alone restores contrast and also corrects colour casts.

Then edge-preserving denoising and USM sharpening run as needed: smooth away the grain of age first, then reinforce outlines softened by scanning, and the photo looks clear again.

FAQ about photo restoration

Can it fix tears and creases?

No. This tool only enhances colour and sharpness digitally. Physical damage, creases, stains or missing areas require manual retouching.

Can it colourise black & white photos?

No. Auto-colourising needs an AI model to guess plausible colours. This tool is purely algorithmic and never invents colour.

The result looks too saturated — what can I do?

Turn off "Auto levels" and keep only denoising and sharpening. You can also fine-tune saturation manually with the Colour Adjust tool.

What scan resolution should I use?

Scan at 300–600 dpi. If the resolution is too low, restoration can only improve colour — it cannot add real detail.
