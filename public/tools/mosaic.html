How to pixelate

  1. Upload an image
  2. To mask part of the image, choose "Selected area only", then drag on the original above to draw a box. You can draw several boxes before applying — on a desktop hold the left mouse button, on a phone use one finger
  3. To mask everything, just pick "Whole image"
  4. Set the block size, click "Apply pixelation", check the coverage, then download

Touch gestures on a phone (for "Selected area only")

Hiding a small detail — a licence plate, a face, a house number — is much easier once you zoom in. All of the gestures below work directly on the image itself, so you never have to move your fingers outside the picture, leave the page or re-upload the file:

  • One finger draws a box. Press and drag on the image to outline the area you want pixelated. One finger always means "draw a box" and never zooms or pans, at any zoom level.
  • Two fingers pinch to zoom. Spread two fingers apart on the image to enlarge it, pinch them together to shrink it back. Zoom runs from 1× to 5× and stays anchored where your fingers are, so the spot you are looking at does not jump away.
  • Two fingers drag to pan. Move both fingers together — up, down, left or right — to bring another part of the enlarged image into view. The image always fills the preview frame, so it can never be dragged off-screen and lost.
  • Switch between them freely. Zoom in with two fingers, draw a precise box with one finger, then pan or pinch back out with two fingers to check the result or pick another area. Repeat as often as you like: a two-finger gesture never draws a box by accident, and lifting one finger after a pinch does not leave a stray box behind.

Boxes are stored as percentages of the original image, not as screen positions. A box drawn while zoomed in is therefore applied to exactly the same spot in the full-resolution result — zooming changes only what you see, never where the pixelation lands. Pinch back to 1× for a full view when you are done; switching to "Whole image" also resets the zoom.

How it works

Pixelation divides the image into small blocks and replaces each block with the average colour of all its pixels. The larger the block, the more pixels are averaged and the more thoroughly detail is destroyed.

Because it averages whole blocks, pixelation is irreversible — the original pixels are overwritten and cannot be recovered from the result, which is exactly why it protects privacy.

For partial masking, the averaging runs only within the normalised coordinates you selected; everything outside is untouched, so you can hide a licence plate while keeping the car intact.

FAQ about pixelation

Can pixelation be reversed?

No. Averaging destroys the original information. Note, though, that small blocks can still be readable — for licence plates use a block size of 20 or more.

Why does drawing a box do nothing?

Make sure "Selected areas only" is selected, then hold the left mouse button and drag on the original. A red dashed box appears; you can draw several.

Can it mask video?

This version handles still images only. For video, export frames and process them, or use dedicated video software.

Does the file get bigger after pixelation?

Not noticeably. Simplified colours in masked areas usually compress better, so PNG files often end up smaller than the original.
