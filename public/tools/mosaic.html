How to pixelate

  1. Upload an image
  2. To mask part of the image, choose "Selected areas only", then hold and drag on the preview to draw a box (you can draw several)
  3. To mask everything, just pick "Whole image"
  4. Set the block size, click "Apply pixelation", check the coverage, then download

How it works

Pixelation divides the image into small blocks and replaces each block with the average colour of all its pixels. The larger the block, the more pixels are averaged and the more thoroughly detail is destroyed.

Because it averages whole blocks, pixelation is irreversible — the original pixels are overwritten and cannot be recovered from the result, which is exactly why it protects privacy.

For partial masking, the averaging runs only within the normalised coordinates you selected; everything outside is untouched, so you can hide a licence plate while keeping the car intact.

Common use cases

It works best for:

Hide licence plates

Cover plates before posting used-car or accident scene photos

Protect faces

People in street shots or group photos who prefer not to appear

Hide document details

Names and phone numbers on ID cards and shipping labels

Cover addresses and receipts

Addresses on delivery slips and access-control screenshots

FAQ about pixelation

Can pixelation be reversed?

No. Averaging destroys the original information. Note, though, that small blocks can still be readable — for licence plates use a block size of 20 or more.

Why does drawing a box do nothing?

Make sure "Selected areas only" is selected, then hold the left mouse button and drag on the original. A red dashed box appears; you can draw several.

Can it mask video?

This version handles still images only. For video, export frames and process them, or use dedicated video software.

Does the file get bigger after pixelation?

Not noticeably. Simplified colours in masked areas usually compress better, so PNG files often end up smaller than the original.
