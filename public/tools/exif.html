How it works

EXIF is structured data embedded in the JPEG header, recording camera settings, capture time and even GPS coordinates. It lives in the APP1 marker segment and does not affect how the picture displays, which is why many people never know it is there.

Reading it means parsing this block as TIFF: determine byte order (big or little endian), locate the IFD (Image File Directory) entries, then look up each tag number to learn what the field means.

Stripping is straightforward: redraw the image onto a Canvas and export. Canvas exports pure pixel data with none of the original metadata blocks, so GPS and camera settings disappear naturally.

FAQ about EXIF

Why can I not read EXIF from my photo?

Likely reasons: the file is PNG or WebP (which usually carry no EXIF); the photo was already compressed and stripped by a platform such as WeChat or Weibo; or it was generated by software rather than a camera.

Does stripping reduce quality?

PNG export is lossless with no change in quality. JPG recompresses at 92% quality — virtually identical to the eye, but strictly one more lossy encode.

Can stripped data be restored?

No. The metadata is discarded entirely and is absent from the exported file. Confirm before you proceed, and keep the original if needed.

Does WeChat strip EXIF?

Platforms like WeChat and Weibo usually compress and strip most metadata on upload, so images relayed through them typically carry no location. Originals sent via cloud storage or email keep everything.
