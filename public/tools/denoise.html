How to reduce noise

  1. Upload a noisy photo
  2. Drag the strength slider — start around 3
  3. Click "Denoise"
  4. Compare with the original; if it went too far, lower the strength and redo

How it works

Noise is random pixel disturbance caused by sensor heat or low light at capture time, showing up as coloured grain across the image.

Plain blurring removes noise but smears face outlines and text edges along with it. This tool uses an edge-preserving filter (a bilateral approach): only neighbouring pixels of similar colour get averaged, while pixels that differ sharply — the edges — get far less weight.

So noise is smoothed thoroughly in flat areas such as sky or walls, while strong edges like eyebrows, hair and letter strokes are preserved.

FAQ about noise reduction

Does denoising make photos blurry?

Too much strength will. This tool preserves edges, but past 8 you still lose detail. Start at 3 and increase until it looks right.

Heavy noise will not clear — what now?

Try scaling the image down slightly first (downscaling itself averages noise away), or run two gentle passes instead of one aggressive one — that usually works better.

How long does it take?

Edge-preserving filtering computes neighbourhood weights per pixel, so a 12-megapixel image may take ten seconds or more. Larger images are slower — that is normal.

Can it remove JPEG compression noise?

It helps. Blocking artefacts from heavy JPEG compression get smoothed too, but severe mosquito noise cannot be fully recovered.
