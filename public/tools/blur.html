How to blur the background

  1. Upload: Drop an image here; under 5 MB is recommended.
  2. Set the blur strength: Drag the strength slider; higher values blur more. Portraits usually suit 10–20; to hide information go above 30.
  3. Choose the blur area: Blur the whole image or keep the subject sharp.
  4. Process and download: Compare before and after, then download when you are happy. PNG is recommended to preserve quality.

Tip: All processing happens in your browser: images are never uploaded to a server. Results live only on this page, so download them promptly.

How it works

Blurring works by averaging each pixel's colour with a surrounding area. The larger the area and the more passes, the blurrier the result. This tool first scales the image down in proportion to the radius, runs several box blur passes on the small image, then smoothly scales it back up.

Why shrink first? Because blurring a large image means visiting a huge number of pixels. Scaling down to a fraction cuts the work by orders of magnitude, and the smoothing applied when scaling back up adds blur of its own — which actually looks closer to the circle of confusion of a real lens. Stacking several box blurs approximates a Gaussian blur mathematically, a standard trick in graphics.

One caveat: blurring is irreversible. Once detail has been averaged away, the sharp original cannot be recovered from the result — always keep your original.

FAQ about background blur

Can a blurred image be sharpened again?
No. Blurring averages neighbouring pixels, so the original detail is gone and cannot be reverse-engineered. Always save the original.
What strength hides a licence plate?
Around 30 or higher is usually safe. The test is whether you can still make out characters when zoomed in — if they remain guessable, increase further or switch to a pixelation tool.
What is the difference between full and background blur?
Full blur softens the subject too and works well as a backdrop. Background-only blur requires identifying the subject; this tool preserves the central area, so it works best when the subject is centred.
Why is processing slow?
Large images are much slower. Use the Resize tool to bring the long edge under 2000 px first, then blur — it will be far quicker.
