How it works

A QR code encodes text into a matrix of black and white modules. A mode indicator marks the data type (numeric, alphanumeric or byte), followed by a character count and the content, padded to a fixed length.

What really lets a damaged code still scan is Reed-Solomon error correction. The original data is split into blocks, each generating extra check codewords, which are interleaved and written together. Even if some modules are obscured or dirty, the decoder can reconstruct the original data from the check codewords.

Error correction levels L, M, Q and H tolerate roughly 7%, 15%, 25% and 30%. The higher the level, the more space check codewords take and the less real content fits — and since a logo covers the centre, H is recommended when adding one.

Generation also picks one of eight mask patterns, choosing the one that distributes black and white most evenly and avoids shapes resembling the finder patterns, which scanners detect most reliably.

FAQ about QR generation

My QR with a logo will not scan — what now?

Set error level to H (about 30% tolerance) and shrink the logo to under 20%. The logo covers central modules, and a higher level compensates better. Square logos also scan more reliably than long ones.

Do coloured QR codes scan?

Yes, as long as contrast between foreground and background is strong. Pale or low-contrast pairs (white modules on yellow, say) confuse scanners — keep the contrast obvious.

How much content fits?

It depends on the error level: about 1000 bytes at L, about 400 at H. Chinese text in UTF-8 costs roughly 3 bytes per character. You will be prompted to shorten if it overflows.

Do generated QR codes expire?

No. A QR code just encodes content into a graphic with no time information; as long as the content (a URL, say) stays valid, it keeps working.

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Generation, logo compositing and download all happen in your browser — the content never leaves your device.
